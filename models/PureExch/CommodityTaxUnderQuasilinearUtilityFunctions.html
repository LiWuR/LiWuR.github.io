Excess Burden of a Commodity Tax under Quasilinear Utility Functions

The \(4 \times 4\) Model

This example is essentially a pure exchange economy, with a tax intermediary added to implement the commodity tax.

Assume the following:

  1. The economy contains four commodities: corn, iron, taxed iron, and tax certificates; and four economic agents: consumer 1, consumer 2, a firm, and the government. Corn is taken as the numeraire commodity.

  2. Consumer 1 has the utility function

    \[ u_1(x_1,x_3) = x_1+\beta_1\left(\alpha_1x_3-\frac12x_3^2\right) \]

    where \(x_1\) denotes corn, \(x_3\) denotes taxed iron, and \(\alpha_1,\beta_1>0\). Consumer 1 is endowed with \(\omega_1\) units of corn.

  3. Consumer 2 has the utility function

    \[ u_2(x_1,x_2) = x_1+\beta_2\left(\alpha_2x_2-\frac12x_2^2\right) \]

    where \(x_1\) denotes corn, \(x_2\) denotes iron, and \(\alpha_2,\beta_2>0\). Consumer 2 is endowed with \(\omega_2\) units of iron. Consumer 1 wishes to purchase iron supplied by consumer 2, and the government imposes a commodity tax on iron.

  4. The firm acts as a tax agency. It uses iron and tax certificates as inputs to produce taxed iron. Under the tax system, consumer 1 must purchase taxed iron from the firm, while consumer 2 must sell iron through the firm.

  5. The government is endowed with one unit of the tax certificate and has the utility function \(u_G=x_1\).

Equilibrium

Suppose the parameter values ensure interior solutions in both the taxed and no-tax equilibria, with positive consumption of corn and iron-related commodities for both consumers and positive equilibrium prices for \(\tau>0\). Consumer 1’s demand for taxed iron is

\[ x_3(p_3) = \alpha_1-\frac{p_3}{\beta_1} \]

and consumer 2’s demand for iron is

\[ x_2(p_2) = \alpha_2-\frac{p_2}{\beta_2} \]

Let the price vector be

\[ \mathbf p:=(1,p_2,p_3,p_4)^\top \]

where \(p_2\) is the price of iron, \(p_3\) is the price of taxed iron, and \(p_4\) is the price of the tax certificate. Let \(y\) denote the firm’s activity level, which is also its output of taxed iron; let \(\mathbf w=(w_1,w_2)^\top\) denote the vector of consumer incomes; let \(u\) denote the government’s utility level; and let \(\tau>0\) denote the ad valorem commodity tax rate.

Consumer 1 purchases \(x_3\) units of taxed iron, so its demand for corn is \(w_1-p_3x_3\). Consumer 2 consumes \(x_2\) units of iron and sells the remaining iron, so its demand for corn is \(w_2-p_2x_2\).

To produce one unit of taxed iron, the firm uses one unit of iron and tax certificates with a total value equal to \(\tau p_2\). Therefore, producing \(y\) units of taxed iron requires

\[ \frac{yp_2\tau}{p_4} \]

units of tax certificates.

The government sells its endowment of tax certificates and spends the resulting income entirely on corn.

The demand matrix is therefore

\[ \mathbf D(\mathbf p,u,\mathbf w,y) := \begin{bmatrix} w_1-p_3x_3 & w_2-p_2x_2 & 0 & u\\ 0 & x_2 & y & 0\\ x_3 & 0 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & \frac{yp_2\tau}{p_4} & 0 \end{bmatrix} \]

and the supply matrix is

\[ \mathbf S(y) := \begin{bmatrix} \omega_1 & 0 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & \omega_2 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & y & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Solving the equality-form wide-sense structural equilibrium model gives

\[ p_2^* = \frac{ \beta_1\beta_2(\alpha_1+\alpha_2-\omega_2) }{ \beta_1+(1+\tau)\beta_2 } \]

\[ p_3^* = (1+\tau)p_2^* \]

and

\[ p_4^* = y^*p_2^*\tau \]

The equilibrium quantity of taxed iron is

\[ y^* = \alpha_1-\frac{p_3^*}{\beta_1} \]

with

\[ x_3^*=y^* \]

and

\[ x_2^* = \alpha_2-\frac{p_2^*}{\beta_2} \]

The equilibrium consumer incomes and government utility level are

\[ w_1^*=\omega_1 \]

\[ w_2^*=p_2^*\omega_2 \]

and

\[ u^*=p_4^* \]

respectively.

The commodity tax generates a wedge between the buyer’s price and the seller’s price:

\[ p_3^*-p_2^* = \tau p_2^* \]

Consider the parameter values

\[ \beta_1=\beta_2=0.05,\qquad \alpha_1=120,\qquad \alpha_2=100 \]

\[ \omega_1=1000,\qquad \omega_2=100,\qquad \tau=1 \]

The analytical solution is

\[ p_2^*=2,\qquad p_3^*=4,\qquad p_4^*=80 \]

and

\[ y^*=40 \]

Hence the equilibrium price vector is

\[ \mathbf p^* = (1,2,4,80)^\top \]

Consumer 1 consumes 840 units of corn and 40 units of taxed iron. Consumer 2 consumes 80 units of corn and 60 units of iron. The government consumes 80 units of corn.

Excess Burden of the Commodity Tax

The equilibrium utilities of the two consumers under the commodity tax rate \(\tau\) are

\[ u_1^*(\tau) = \omega_1-p_3^*x_3^* + \beta_1 \left( \alpha_1x_3^* -\frac12(x_3^*)^2 \right) \]

and

\[ u_2^*(\tau) = p_2^*(\omega_2-x_2^*) + \beta_2 \left( \alpha_2x_2^* -\frac12(x_2^*)^2 \right) \]

The government’s equilibrium utility is

\[ u_G^*(\tau) = u^* = p_4^* = y^*p_2^*\tau \]

Since the three utility functions are normalized in units of corn, define the utilitarian social welfare measure under the commodity tax rate \(\tau\) as

\[ W(\tau) := u_1^*(\tau) + u_2^*(\tau) + u_G^*(\tau) \]

Let \(W(0)\) denote social welfare in the no-tax equilibrium. The excess burden of the commodity tax is

\[ W(0)-W(\tau) \]

which can be calculated as

\[ W(0)-W(\tau) = \frac{ \beta_1^2\beta_2^2\tau^2 (\alpha_1+\alpha_2-\omega_2)^2 }{ 2(\beta_1+\beta_2) \left[\beta_1+\beta_2(1+\tau)\right]^2 } \]

Using the equilibrium price \(p_2^*\), this can be written more simply as

\[ W(0)-W(\tau) = \frac{(\tau p_2^*)^2} {2(\beta_1+\beta_2)} \]

The reduction in the equilibrium quantity of traded iron relative to the no-tax equilibrium is

\[ y^*(0)-y^*(\tau) = \frac{\tau p_2^*}{\beta_1+\beta_2} \]

while the commodity-tax wedge is

\[ p_3^*-p_2^* = \tau p_2^* \]

Hence the excess burden can also be expressed as

\[ W(0)-W(\tau) = \frac12 (p_3^*-p_2^*) \left[y^*(0)-y^*(\tau)\right] \]

which is the area of the excess-burden triangle generated by the commodity tax.

For the numerical parameter values above, the no-tax equilibrium quantity is

\[ y^*(0)=60 \]

while the equilibrium quantity under \(\tau=1\) is

\[ y^*(1)=40 \]

The commodity-tax wedge is

\[ p_3^*-p_2^*=2 \]

and therefore the excess burden is

\[ \frac12(60-40)(4-2)=20 \]

Equivalently,

\[ W(0)=1430,\qquad W(1)=1410 \]

so that

\[ W(0)-W(1)=20 \]

When \(\tau=0\), the tax certificate has no economic role and its equilibrium price is zero, while the term \(\frac{yp_2\tau}{p_4}\) in the demand matrix becomes indeterminate. The no-tax equilibrium should therefore be represented without the tax-certificate commodity, or equivalently interpreted through the limit as \(\tau\to0^+\) when welfare is considered.
