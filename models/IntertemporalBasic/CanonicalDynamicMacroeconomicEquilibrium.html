Finite-Horizon Canonical Dynamic Macroeconomic Equilibrium

Equilibrium without Tail Adjustment: A \(3\times 3\) Model

Assume the following (see Torres, 2016, chap. 2):

  1. The economy has \(n_p\) periods and \(n_p-1\) production intervals. A product firm operating in period \(t\) starts production at the beginning of the period and obtains its output at the end of period \(t\), or equivalently at the beginning of period \(t+1\). Commodities dated at different periods are treated as different commodities.

  2. The economy contains three types of dated commodities: products, labor, and capital rentals. Product firms and capital-rental firms operate in periods \(t=1,\ldots,n_p-1\), and there is one intertemporal consumer. The product also serves as capital.

  3. The product firm operating in period \(t\) rents capital \(k^{(t)}\) and employs labor \(x^{(t)}\) to produce the product available in period \(t+1\) according to

\[ y^{(t+1)} = \alpha^{(t)} \left(k^{(t)}\right)^{\beta_1} \left(x^{(t)}\right)^{1-\beta_1} \]

where \(\beta_1=0.35\) and \(\alpha^{(t)}=1\) in the benchmark case.

  1. The capital-rental firm purchases the current product as capital, supplies its use to the product firm, and carries the undepreciated capital into the next period. Thus, an activity level of \(k^{(t)}\) requires \(k^{(t)}\) units of \(\mathrm{product}^{(t)}\) and supplies \(k^{(t)}\) units of \(\mathrm{capital_rental}^{(t)}\) together with \((1-\delta)k^{(t)}\) units of \(\mathrm{product}^{(t+1)}\), where \(\delta=0.06\). Combining the two production activities, the total product supply generated in period \(t\) and available in period \(t+1\) is \(y^{(t+1)}+(1-\delta)k^{(t)}\).

  2. The representative consumer initially owns \(k^{(1)}=286.6341\) units of product and is endowed with \(\omega=100\) units of labor in each production period. Under the post-payment wage assumption, labor supplied in period \(t\) and product consumed in period \(t+1\) enter the same single-period utility function. Letting \(\ell^{(t)}\) denote leisure,

\[ u^{(t)} = \left(c^{(t+1)}\right)^{\beta_2} \left(\ell^{(t)}\right)^{1-\beta_2} \]

where \(\beta_2=0.4\). In equilibrium, labor-market clearing implies \(\ell^{(t)}=\omega-x^{(t)}\).

The subjective discount factor is \(\varGamma=0.97\).

  1. Without tail adjustment, the intertemporal utility is formed from the single-period utility levels with relative weights

\[ 1,\varGamma,\varGamma^2,\ldots,\varGamma^{n_p-2} \]

Since both the within-period and intertemporal elasticities of substitution equal one, the nested utility function can be represented by a single CESSpec. For a sufficiently long horizon, the middle part of the equilibrium path approaches the steady state, although the final periods are affected by the finite-horizon tail effect.

Equilibrium with Tail Adjustment

To mitigate the finite-horizon tail effect, an additional demand for the terminal product is introduced alongside the ordinary single-period utility components. Let \(\eta>0\) denote its endogenous weight. The intertemporal utility structure is

\[ U \sim \prod_{t=1}^{n_p-1} \left(u^{(t)}\right)^{\varGamma^{t-1}} \left(c^{(n_p)}\right)^\eta \]

The ordinary single-period preferences and discount factors remain unchanged. When the nested Cobb–Douglas utility is represented by a single CESSpec, the weight on the terminal product becomes \(\beta_2\varGamma^{n_p-2}+\eta\).

Since capital is the state variable of the model, \(\eta\) is paired with the tail condition

\[ k^{(n_p-2)} = k^{(n_p-1)} \]

Thus, the terminal weight is determined endogenously so that the last two capital stocks coincide.

With \(n_p=20\), the steady-state benchmark is approximately

\[ k^*=286.6, \qquad x^*=36.04, \qquad y^*=74.47, \qquad c^*=57.27 \]

The tail-adjusted equilibrium gives

\[ \eta=1.157, \qquad \frac{\eta}{\varGamma^{18}} \approx2.002 \]

where \(\varGamma^{18}\) is the discount weight on the last ordinary single-period utility component. The last two capital stocks are

\[ k^{(18)} = k^{(19)} = 286.6 \]
