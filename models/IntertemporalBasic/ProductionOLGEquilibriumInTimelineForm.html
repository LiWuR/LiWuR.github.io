Production OLG Equilibrium in Timeline Form

The \(2\times2\) Model

Consider a finite-horizon overlapping-generations economy with production, closely related to the classical production OLG model of Diamond (1965). The model is represented in timeline form using dated commodities.

Assume the following:

  1. The economy contains two commodity types, product and labor, and two economic-agent types, firms and consumers. There are \(n_g\) consumer generations and \(n_p=n_g+2\) periods. Hence there are \(n_p\) dated products and \(n_p-1\) dated labor commodities.

  2. Consumer generation \(g\) lives for three periods and consumes products in periods \(g\), \(g+1\), and \(g+2\). Its Cobb–Douglas utility function is

\[ u_g = \left(c_g^{(g)}\right)^{\beta_1} \left(c_g^{(g+1)}\right)^{\beta_2} \left(c_g^{(g+2)}\right)^{\beta_3} \]

where \(\beta_1+\beta_2+\beta_3=1\).

A normal generation supplies \(\omega_1\) units of labor in the first period of its life and \(\omega_2\) units in the second period, while \(\omega_3=0\). The first generation additionally owns \(y_1\) units of the initial product. Later generations own labor only.

  1. Firm \(t\) uses the product and labor available in period \(t\) to produce the product supplied in period \(t+1\). Let \(k^{(t)}\) and \(x^{(t)}\) denote its product and labor inputs. Its Cobb–Douglas production function is

\[ y^{(t+1)} = \alpha \left(k^{(t)}\right)^{\beta_f} \left(x^{(t)}\right)^{1-\beta_f} \] The product input \(k^{(t)}\) can be interpreted as the capital used in period \(t\).

  1. Let \(\varGamma_l\) denote the population growth factor and \(\varGamma_p\) the steady-state growth factor of dated product prices. The total labor supply available to a firm in a stationary demographic configuration is

\[ l = \omega_1 + \frac{\omega_2}{\varGamma_l} + \frac{\omega_3}{\varGamma_l^2} \]

For a consumer born in period \(t\), lifetime labor income measured relative to the labor price in period \(t\) is

\[ w = \omega_1 + \omega_2\varGamma_p + \omega_3\varGamma_p^2 \]

The steady-state equilibrium therefore satisfies

\[ \frac{ \left(1-\beta_f\varGamma_p\varGamma_l\right)l }{ (1-\beta_f)\varGamma_p\varGamma_l } = w \left( \beta_1 + \frac{\beta_2}{\varGamma_p\varGamma_l} + \frac{\beta_3} {\varGamma_p^2\varGamma_l^2} \right) \]

which determines the possible steady-state values of \(\varGamma_p\).

  1. Given any positive solution \(\varGamma_p\) of the steady-state equation, steady-state output is

\[ y = \alpha^{\frac{1}{1-\beta_f}} \left( \beta_f\varGamma_p \right)^{\frac{\beta_f}{1-\beta_f}} l \]

A useful property of the steady-state equation is that

\[ \varGamma_{p,h} = \frac{1}{\varGamma_l} \]

is always a solution. Under this value, \(w=l\) and the expenditure-share term on the right-hand side sums to one. The corresponding steady-state output is

\[ y_h = \alpha^{\frac{1}{1-\beta_f}} \left( \frac{\beta_f}{\varGamma_l} \right)^{\frac{\beta_f}{1-\beta_f}} l \]

Under \(\omega_1>0\), \(\beta_3>0\), and \(0<\beta_f<1\), the steady-state equation generically has at least one additional positive solution besides \(\varGamma_{p,h}=1/\varGamma_l\). Hence, except for knife-edge parameter values at which positive roots coincide, the model has multiple positive steady-state equilibria.

Numerical Example

For the numerical example, let

\[ n_g=50, \qquad \varGamma_l=1, \qquad \alpha=2, \qquad \beta_f=0.2 \]

\[ (\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3) = (0.1,0.1,0.8), \qquad (\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3) = (50,50,0) \]

and let the first generation own \(y_1=8\) units of the initial product.

For these parameters, \(l=100\), and the steady-state equation can be reduced to

\[ (\varGamma_p-1) \left( \varGamma_p^2+8\varGamma_p-8 \right) = 0 \]

It therefore has two positive solutions. The first is the general steady-state solution

\[ \varGamma_{p,h}=1 \]

with corresponding output

\[ y_h \approx159.1 \]

The second positive solution is

\[ \varGamma_{p,l} = -4+2\sqrt6 \approx0.8990 \]

with corresponding output

\[ y_l \approx154.9 \]

Thus, for the numerical parameterization, the model has two positive steady-state equilibria. The computed finite-horizon path converges in its interior to the lower-output steady state associated with \(\varGamma_{p,l}\).
