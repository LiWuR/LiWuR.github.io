Intertemporal Equilibrium with Production and Endogenous Interest Rates

The \(3\times2\) Model

In structural equilibrium models, money is usually treated as an ad valorem claim, and the interest rate is usually treated as an ad valorem claim rate (Li, 2019, chap. 7). This example considers a finite-horizon intertemporal equilibrium with production and endogenous interest rates. There are three types of dated commodities—product, labor, and money—and two types of economic agents—firm and consumer.

Assume the following:

  1. The economy operates for \(n_p\) periods. In each period there are three dated commodities: product \(i\), labor \(i\), and money \(i\), where \(i=1,\ldots,n_p\). Thus, there are \(3n_p\) dated commodities. There is one firm activity in each period and one consumer. Product 1 is the numeraire.
  2. In period \(i\), the firm uses labor \(l_i\) to produce product \(i\) according to

\[ y_i=\alpha_i l_i, \qquad \alpha_i>0 \]

Let \(y_i\) also denote the firm’s activity level, measured by output. Thus, producing \(y_i\) units requires \(y_i/\alpha_i\) units of labor. Before production, the firm presells its product and uses money \(i\) to purchase labor \(i\).

  1. The consumer has the intertemporal Cobb–Douglas utility function

\[ u=\prod_{i=1}^{n_p}c_i^{\beta_i} \]

where \(c_i\) denotes consumption of product \(i\). The expenditure shares are strictly decreasing over time,

\[ \beta_1>\beta_2>\cdots>\beta_{n_p}>0, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n_p}\beta_i=1 \]

The consumer is endowed with \(\omega_i>0\) units of labor \(i\). The decreasing-share assumption will ensure strictly positive equilibrium interest rates in all nonterminal periods. 4. The consumer is endowed with one unit of money in each period. Let \(p_{m_i}\) denote the price of money \(i\). We restrict attention to equilibria with strictly positive nonterminal interest rates,

\[ r_i>0, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

while the terminal condition is

\[ r_{n_p}=+\infty \]

For the terminal period, define the finite interest-inclusive product price

\[ \tilde p_{n_p} := \lim_{r_{n_p}\rightarrow+\infty} (1+r_{n_p})p_{n_p} \]

This variable allows the terminal demands to be written directly without introducing an infinite interest rate into the equilibrium system.

For the three-period case, let

\[ \mathbf p = \left( 1,p_2,p_3, p_{l_1},p_{l_2},p_{l_3}, p_{m_1},p_{m_2},p_{m_3} \right)^\top \]

let \(\mathbf y=(y_1,y_2,y_3)^\top\) denote firm activity levels, and let \(w\) denote consumer income. The consumer’s income is the value of the labor and money endowments,

\[ w = \sum_{i=1}^3p_{l_i}\omega_i + \sum_{i=1}^3p_{m_i} \]

With rows representing product 1–3, labor 1–3, and money 1–3, and columns representing the three firms and the consumer, respectively, the demand matrix is

\[ \mathbf D(\mathbf p,\mathbf y,w,r_1,r_2,\tilde p_3) = \begin{bmatrix} 0&0&0&\dfrac{\beta_1w}{1+r_1}\\[6pt] 0&0&0&\dfrac{\beta_2w}{(1+r_2)p_2}\\[6pt] 0&0&0&\dfrac{\beta_3w}{\tilde p_3}\\[6pt] \dfrac{y_1}{\alpha_1}&0&0&0\\[6pt] 0&\dfrac{y_2}{\alpha_2}&0&0\\[6pt] 0&0&\dfrac{y_3}{\alpha_3}&0\\[6pt] \dfrac{r_1p_{l_1}y_1}{\alpha_1p_{m_1}}&0&0& \dfrac{\beta_1wr_1}{(1+r_1)p_{m_1}}\\[8pt] 0&\dfrac{r_2p_{l_2}y_2}{\alpha_2p_{m_2}}&0& \dfrac{\beta_2wr_2}{(1+r_2)p_{m_2}}\\[8pt] 0&0&0&\dfrac{\beta_3w}{p_{m_3}} \end{bmatrix} \]

and the supply matrix is

\[ \mathbf S(\mathbf y) = \begin{bmatrix} y_1&0&0&0\\ 0&y_2&0&0\\ 0&0&y_3&0\\ 0&0&0&\omega_1\\ 0&0&0&\omega_2\\ 0&0&0&\omega_3\\ 0&0&0&1\\ 0&0&0&1\\ 0&0&0&1 \end{bmatrix} \]

The terminal firm’s money demand is zero in the limiting representation. The terminal consumer’s product and money demands are therefore written directly in terms of \(\tilde p_3\) and \(p_{m_3}\).

For an arbitrary period \(i\), labor-market clearing gives

\[ \frac{y_i^*}{\alpha_i}=\omega_i \]

and hence

\[ y_i^*=c_i^*=\alpha_i\omega_i \]

For a nonterminal period, product-market clearing then implies

\[ p_i^* = \frac{\beta_iw^*} {(1+r_i^*)\alpha_i\omega_i}, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

where \(p_1^*=1\). Hence,

\[ w^* = \frac{\alpha_1(1+r_1^*)\omega_1}{\beta_1} \]

The firm’s unit revenue-expenditure balance condition gives

\[ p_{l_i}^* = \frac{\alpha_i p_i^*}{1+r_i^*}, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

For the terminal period, product-market clearing instead gives

\[ \tilde p_{n_p}^* = \frac{\beta_{n_p}w^*} {\alpha_{n_p}\omega_{n_p}} \]

while

\[ p_{n_p}^*\rightarrow0, \qquad p_{l_{n_p}}^*\rightarrow0 \]

For a nonterminal period, the firm’s and consumer’s equilibrium money holdings required for transactions are

\[ h_i^{f*} = \frac{r_i^*p_{l_i}^*y_i^*} {\alpha_i p_{m_i}^*} = \frac{p_{l_i}^*\omega_i r_i^*}{p_{m_i}^*} \]

and

\[ h_i^{c*} = \frac{\beta_iw^*r_i^*} {(1+r_i^*)p_{m_i}^*} \]

Money-market clearing, \(h_i^{f*}+h_i^{c*}=1\), gives

\[ p_{m_i}^* = \beta_iw^* \frac{ r_i^*(r_i^*+2) }{ (1+r_i^*)^2 }, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

Under the terminal condition,

\[ p_{m_{n_p}}^*=\beta_{n_p}w^* \]

The terminal firm’s money holdings approach zero, while the consumer holds the entire unit of terminal money.

Analytical Solution for the Endogenous Interest Rates

The endogenous interest rates are determined by the equilibrium values of dated money. Define the tail value of money prices by

\[ v_i := \sum_{j=i}^{n_p}p_{m_j}^* \]

For each nonterminal period, the ad valorem claim-rate relation is

\[ r_i^* = \frac{p_{m_i}^*}{v_{i+1}}, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

Since

\[ v_i=p_{m_i}^*+v_{i+1} \]

we have

\[ 1+r_i^* = \frac{v_i}{v_{i+1}} \]

Normalize the tail value by consumer income:

\[ g_i := \frac{v_i}{w^*} \]

The terminal value is

\[ g_{n_p}=\beta_{n_p} \]

and

\[ 1+r_i^* = \frac{g_i}{g_{i+1}} \]

Using

\[ \frac{p_{m_i}^*}{w^*} = r_i^*g_{i+1} \]

together with the money-market expression gives

\[ g_i^2-\beta_i g_i-\beta_i g_{i+1}=0 \]

Thus, the positive root yields the backward recursion

\[ g_i = \frac{ \beta_i+ \sqrt{ \beta_i^2+4\beta_i g_{i+1} } }{2}, \qquad i=n_p-1,\ldots,1 \]

with

\[ g_{n_p}=\beta_{n_p} \]

Once the sequence \(g_i\) is obtained, the nonterminal equilibrium interest rates follow from

\[ r_i^* = \frac{g_i}{g_{i+1}}-1, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

The decreasing-share assumption guarantees that these interest rates are strictly positive. To see this, note first that

\[ g_{n_p}=\beta_{n_p}<2\beta_{n_p} \]

Suppose \(g_{i+1}<2\beta_{i+1}\). Since \(\beta_i>\beta_{i+1}\),

\[ g_{i+1}<2\beta_i \]

Using

\[ g_i^2-\beta_i g_i-\beta_i g_{i+1}=0 \]

and evaluating the left-hand side at \(g_{i+1}\) and \(2\beta_i\) shows that the positive root satisfies

\[ g_{i+1}<g_i<2\beta_i \]

This establishes the induction step. Proceeding backward therefore gives

\[ g_1>g_2>\cdots>g_{n_p}>0 \]

and hence

\[ r_i^*>0, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

Thus, for any finite \(n_p\), the backward recursion directly determines the strictly positive nonterminal equilibrium interest rates under the maintained assumption \(\beta_1>\beta_2>\cdots>\beta_{n_p}>0\). The productivity parameters \(\alpha_i\) and labor endowments \(\omega_i\) affect equilibrium quantities and prices but do not enter the interest-rate recursion.

Common Nonterminal Interest Rate

A useful special case imposes a common nonterminal equilibrium interest rate,

\[ r_i^*=r>0, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

Then

\[ g_i=(1+r)g_{i+1} \]

and the share parameters satisfy

\[ \beta_i = \beta_{n_p} \frac{ (1+r)^{n_p-i+1} }{ r+2 }, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

Together with \(\sum_{i=1}^{n_p}\beta_i=1\), this determines the complete sequence of shares. Under the maintained decreasing-share assumption, the common-rate specification must also satisfy

\[ \beta_{n_p-1}>\beta_{n_p} \]

which, using the expression above, is equivalent to

\[ \frac{(1+r)^2}{r+2}>1 \]

or

\[ r>\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2} \]

For earlier periods, \(\beta_i/\beta_{i+1}=1+r>1\), so this terminal-adjacent condition is sufficient for the entire sequence to be strictly decreasing. In particular, when \(r=1\),

\[ \beta_i = \frac{ 2^{n_p-i+1} }{ 2^{n_p+1}-1 }, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

and

\[ \beta_{n_p} = \frac{3}{2^{n_p+1}-1} \]

which gives

\[ r_1^*=\cdots=r_{n_p-1}^*=1 \]

Numerical Example

Consider the three-period case with

\[ \beta_1=\frac8{15}, \qquad \beta_2=\frac4{15}, \qquad \beta_3=\frac15 \]

and

\[ \alpha_1=\alpha_2=\alpha_3=1, \qquad \omega_1=\omega_2=\omega_3=100 \]

The recursive solution gives

\[ r_1^*=r_2^*=1, \qquad r_3=+\infty \]

Consumer income is

\[ w^*=375 \]

The terminal interest-inclusive product price is

\[ \tilde p_3^* = \frac{\beta_3w^*}{\alpha_3\omega_3} = \frac34 \]

and the equilibrium commodity price vector, interpreted in the limiting sense for the terminal product and labor prices, is

\[ \mathbf p^* = \left( 1,\frac12,0, \frac12,\frac14,0, 150,75,75 \right)^\top \]

Indeed,

\[ w^* = \frac12(100) + \frac14(100) + 150+75+75 = 375 \]

The equilibrium output and consumption quantities are

\[ y_i^*=c_i^*=100, \qquad i=1,2,3 \]

The firms’ money holdings are

\[ (h_1^{f*},h_2^{f*},h_3^{f*}) = \left( \frac13,\frac13,0 \right) \]

and the consumer’s money holdings are

\[ (h_1^{c*},h_2^{c*},h_3^{c*}) = \left( \frac23,\frac23,1 \right) \]

Hence, every dated money market clears.

The equilibrium demand and supply matrices are

\[ \mathbf D^* = \begin{bmatrix} 0&0&0&100\\ 0&0&0&100\\ 0&0&0&100\\ 100&0&0&0\\ 0&100&0&0\\ 0&0&100&0\\ \frac13&0&0&\frac23\\ 0&\frac13&0&\frac23\\ 0&0&0&1 \end{bmatrix} \]

and

\[ \mathbf S^* = \begin{bmatrix} 100&0&0&0\\ 0&100&0&0\\ 0&0&100&0\\ 0&0&0&100\\ 0&0&0&100\\ 0&0&0&100\\ 0&0&0&1\\ 0&0&0&1\\ 0&0&0&1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Numerical Solution

The model can be solved using the GEMB framework for an arbitrary number of periods \(n_p\). The variables \(r_1,\ldots,r_{n_p-1}\) are determined jointly with commodity prices and agent activity levels. The terminal infinite interest rate is not introduced directly. Instead, the program uses the finite terminal interest-inclusive product price

\[ \tilde p_{n_p} = \lim_{r_{n_p}\rightarrow+\infty} (1+r_{n_p})p_{n_p} \]

A ConditionAgentSpec introduces \(r_1,\ldots,r_{n_p-1}\) and \(\tilde p_{n_p}\) as endogenous variables. The interest-rate relation is

\[ r_i = \frac{ p_{m_i} }{ \displaystyle\sum_{j=i+1}^{n_p}p_{m_j} }, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

For numerical implementation, it can equivalently be written without division as

\[ r_i \sum_{j=i+1}^{n_p}p_{m_j} -p_{m_i} =0, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

For a nonterminal firm, an activity level \(y_i\) implies labor demand \(y_i/\alpha_i\) and money holdings

\[ h_i^f = \frac{ r_i p_{l_i}y_i }{ \alpha_i p_{m_i} }, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

The terminal firm is represented by the limiting form associated with \(r_{n_p}\rightarrow+\infty\), so its money holdings approach zero.

Given income \(w\), the consumer’s nonterminal product demand and money holdings are

\[ c_i = \frac{ \beta_iw }{ (1+r_i)p_i } \]

and

\[ h_i^c = \frac{ r_i\beta_iw }{ (1+r_i)p_{m_i} }, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_p-1 \]

The terminal demands are represented by the finite limiting expressions

\[ c_{n_p} = \frac{ \beta_{n_p}w }{ \tilde p_{n_p} } \]

and

\[ h_{n_p}^c = \frac{ \beta_{n_p}w }{ p_{m_{n_p}} } \]

The program can generate the common-rate share parameterization above for arbitrary \(n_p\) and solve the resulting equilibrium system numerically. Under the maintained decreasing-share assumption, a common rate should satisfy \(r>(\sqrt5-1)/2\); the numerical example uses \(r=1\).
